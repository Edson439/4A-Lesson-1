Vowels


Learning goals: Students will learn the names of the letters in the English alphabet and be introduced to English as a non-phonetic language, as well as to phonics.

  • Step 1. Explain to your students that they will learn the English alphabet, including its 5 vowel letters and 21 consonant letters.

    You may explain the following:

    As you all know, the color chart shows the sounds we use in English. Now we’re going to connect those sounds to written English. First, we’re going to learn the letters of the English alphabet. Do you know how many letters there are? (Let students guess.)

    That’s right—there are 26 letters. The English alphabet has 5 vowel letters and 21 consonant letters. We’ll start with the vowels. I like to call them “open” letters because your mouth stays open when you pronounce them.

    Now, let’s look at these letters and learn their names.

  • Step 2. Teach the vowel letters.

    Explain to your students that they will learn the five vowel letters.

    1. a. Select the first letter (a) from the lower bar.

    2. b. Click on the blank circle to reveal the color chart sound associated with the letter name.

    3. c. Select a student and ask him/her to say the name of the letter at least twice. Reinforce the student’s participation by confirming the letter name.

    4. d. Continue in the same fashion with different students until you have covered all five vowel letters.

    a e i o u

  • Step 3. Help students review all the vowels randomly by saying the letter names.

    1. a. Select a student and click on the button to shuffle the letters.

    2. b. Have the student pronounce each letter. If the pronunciation is correct, provide positive feedback. If the pronunciation is incorrect, follow the correction process: Click on the letter and have the student say the corresponding color chart sound to recall the letter name.

    3. c. Repeat the same procedure with two more students for the remaining sets of letters.

Consonants


Learning goals: Students will learn the names of the letters in the English alphabet and be introduced to English as a non-phonetic language, as well as to phonics.

  • Step 1. Explain to your students that they will now learn the consonant letters in English.

    You may explain the following:

    Consonants are the other letters in the alphabet. They are different from vowels because your mouth is not completely open when you say them. Your lips, tongue, or teeth stop the air in some way. Most words use both vowels and consonants, and we need consonants to make clear and complete words.

    Now, let’s practice the names of these letters.

  • Step 2. Teach the consonant letters: m-s-t-l-n

Phonics


Learning goals: Students will learn the names of the letters in the English alphabet and be introduced to English as a non-phonetic language, as well as to phonics.

  • Step 1. Briefly explain to your students that English is a non-phonetic language and introduce phonics.

    You may explain it like this using the whiteboard:

    Guys, languages can be divided into two big groups: phonetic languages and non-phonetic languages. Let me explain.

    In phonetic languages, we usually pronounce words the same way we write them. For example, in Spanish, when we see this word… (write the word “mesa” on the whiteboard without pronouncing it), we know how to say it just by looking at it: “me-sa” (pronounce the word out loud).

    That’s a phonetic language.

    But, non-phonetic languages are different. In non-phonetic languages, words are not always pronounced the way we write them. In English, for example, if we see this word… (write the word “table” on the whiteboard without pronouncing it), we might think the pronunciation is “ta-ble” (pronounce the word out loud as suggested in Spanish)…

    …but no! The correct pronunciation of this word is “table” (pronounce the word out loud correctly).

    You see? The pronunciation is different from the letters.

    That’s why in English, it’s important to learn, little by little, how letters and sounds work together.

    Let’s see how this works…

  • Step 2. Introduce short vowel sounds.

    Let’s start with some very important sounds in English: vowel sounds. We find these sounds in many words, so they are very important. We’ll begin with short vowel sounds… ready?

  • Step 3. Introduce and practice the short vowel sound for the letter “a.”

    1. a. Show the first slide in the section “Phonics” and have a student say the name of the letter.

      Teacher: Alex, what is the name of this letter?
      Student: "a"
      Teacher: Great!

    1. b. Explain that, although the name of the letter is “a” (sound ) sometimes it is pronounced differently.

      Teacher: Guys, the name of this letter is “a”, but… sometimes it is pronounced differently.

    1. c. Select a student and have him pronounce sound .

      Teacher: Andrea, what is this sound? (as you reveal the sound by clicking on the blank circle)
      Student: “ah”
      Teacher: Very good! Again please.
      Student: “ah”
      Teacher: Excellent! The name of this letter is “a” but sometimes we pronounce it “ah”.

    1. d. Briefly explain the phonics pattern for pronouncing letter “a” with sound .

      Teacher: So, when do we pronounce the letter “a” with the sound “ah”? Here is one rule:
      Teacher: When the letter “a” is between two consonants in a short word, it usually sounds like “ah”
      Teacher: Here is an example:

    1. e. Show the next slide to practice spelling.

      mat

      Teacher: Mario, look at this word. Now, please tell me the letters one by one. What is the first letter?
      Student: “m”
      Teacher: The second letter…
      Student: “a”
      Teacher: and the last letter…
      Student: “t”
      Teacher: Excellent! When we read a word letter by letter, we call this action “spell”, ok?
      Teacher: So, Mario please, spell this word for me.
      Student: m - a - t.
      Teacher: Amazing!

    1. f. Show the next slide to explain visually how the phonics pattern works.


      Teacher: Now, the letters of this word are m, a, and t.
      Teacher: As you can see, we have the letter “a” between two consonants.
      Teacher: So, we don’t pronounce this word “meyt”.
      Teacher: How do we pronounce it?… (select a student randomly to say the correct pronunciation)
      Student: mat
      Teacher: Exactly! We pronounce it “mat”.
